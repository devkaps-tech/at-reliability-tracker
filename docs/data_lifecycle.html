AT Reliability Tracker

How a bus update becomes a punctuality score

Every piece of data in the project, where it is stored, what changes it, and how long it is kept. Data moves top to bottom: AT sends it, we save it untouched, clean it, score it against the timetable, check it, and only then show it.

A real example from 13 Oct 2026

Follow one train arrival through the pipeline

A train on route S-C arrived at Newmarket station (platform 2) at 9:28:45 pm. Here is that one fact at each stage.

1 · Raw, as AT sent it

raw/realtime/date=2026-10-13/222549+1300.json.gz
"trip_id": "257-870005-72360-…",
"start_date": "20261013",
"stop_time_update": {
  "stop_sequence": 23,
  "stop_id": "9103-0e65d9b8",
  "arrival": {
    "time": 1791880125,
    "delay": -75 } },
"timestamp": 1791880136

Times are seconds since 1970 in UTC. We received this 57 minutes after it happened, which is still inside the 2-hour freshness limit.

2 · Clean

data/clean/stop_observations/service_date=2026-10-13/
service_date   2026-10-13
stop_sequence  23
event          arrival
event_time     21:28:45 NZDT
vehicle_id     59208

Converted to Auckland time. The same report shows up in several polls; only one copy is kept.

3 · Scored

data/clean/stop_arrivals/service_date=2026-10-13/
scheduled  21:30:00  (timetable)
actual     21:28:45
delay_s    -75
AT's delay -75   ✓ agrees
time_band  evening

75 seconds early, which is more than 1 minute, so the label is “early”.

4 · Daily result

warehouse.duckdb · daily_route_metrics
route S-C · train · AT Metro
13 Oct · 9 pm hour
stops_scored  2
early         1   ← this one
on_time       1
late          0

Counts are stored rather than percentages, so days, hours and routes can be added up correctly later.

Rules

Why you can trust a number on the dashboard

  • Raw is never edited. Every later table can be rebuilt from it, so a bug fix means re-running days, not losing them.
  • Every result says where it came from: the timetable version it was scored against and the run_id that made it.
  • Re-running a day adds a new run. Nothing is overwritten. The dashboard shows the newest run that passed its checks.
  • The quality gate. Two checks can stop a day being published: at least 95% of live trips found in the timetable, and at most 1% of delays beyond ±2 hours. Three more only warn: polls received, routes covered, and agreement with AT's own delay figure.
  • Time is handled once, carefully. Moments are stored exactly and shown in Auckland time. A trip timetabled at 25:10 belongs to the previous day's service. Times on daylight-saving days are counted from noon minus 12 hours, the way the timetable standard defines them.
